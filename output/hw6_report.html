Documents that fooled the agent

Out of 12, by defense setup. Code alone and prompt alone each stop all 5. Design alone stops tool misuse, not fake facts.

Code layerGives the model only what a person would see. Hidden HTML, tiny, white or off-page PDF text, unseen email parts and invisible characters are removed first.
Design layersend_email and approve_payment need a human sign-off. Code makes the decision from the handbook and writes the reason, so no link can slip in.
Prompt layerTells the model the document is untrusted: “corrections” never override the quote, and conflicts are noted without repeating the fake value.

Recommendation to the store manager

Recommendation

Yes, turn it on for real vendor mail, as a recommender only, with the buyer making the final call.

  • It’s accurate on the documents with an answer key. With defenses, 12 of 12 practice decisions and 60 of 60 fields are right, including all 4 real recommends. No good quote was refused or blanked.
  • It can’t spend money or send mail on its own. Payment and email requests always wait for a person, and none was ever attempted on a real document.
  • Keep the cache healthy. Leave the handbook at the start of the prompt and never put a timestamp or ID before it. That alone is the difference between about $11 and about $86 a month.
  • Start with a one-month pilot. The buyer checks every “recommend” against the quote, plus a sample of rejections. The exam documents have no answer key, so real-world accuracy isn’t proven yet.
  • Known limit: attacks a person can see, such as a visible fake “correction”, rely on the prompt layer alone. Review flagged “conflicting text” reasons by hand.
